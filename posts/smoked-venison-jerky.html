Smoked Venison Jerky

🔥 Camp Cook Kitchen — Tag Soup • April 25, 2026

Thin-cut venison backstrap, smoke-low and slow, finished with a teriyaki glaze. This isn't the gas station stuff — this is the jerky worth getting up early for. The kind that disappears before you make it back to camp.

🫏 What You'll Need

2 lbs venison backstrap (or eye of round) • ¼ cup soy sauce • 2 tbsp Worcestershire • 1 tbsp honey • 1 tbsp brown sugar • 1 tsp black pepper • 1 tsp garlic powder • 1 tsp onion powder • ½ tsp smoked paprika • ¼ tsp cayenne (optional) • 1 tbsp olive oil

Start by putting your venison in the freezer for 45 minutes — just firm enough to slice thin against the grain. Partial freeze makes all the difference for clean, even slices. While you wait, whisk together the soy sauce, Worcestershire, honey, brown sugar, black pepper, garlic powder, onion powder, smoked paprika, and cayenne in a bowl until the sugar fully dissolves.

Slice the venison about ¼ inch thick — as uniform as you can get it. The goal is maximum surface area for smoke penetration. Toss the slices in the marinade and let them sit for at least 2 hours, ideally overnight in the fridge. The longer they soak, the deeper the flavor penetrates. When you're ready to smoke, pull the slices and let any excess marinade drip off — too much liquid will cause the smoke to condense rather than penetrate.

Fire up your smoker to 160°F and hold it there. Hickory is the classic choice, but cherry wood adds a subtle fruitiness that pairs beautifully with game. Lay your slices directly on the smoker grates, making sure they don't overlap. Smoke until the exterior looks tacky and dry, about 3 to 4 hours. You want them pliable but not soft — there's a fine line between done and overdone.

For the teriyaki finish, combine ¼ cup soy sauce, 2 tbsp honey, and 1 tbsp rice vinegar in a small saucepan. Simmer for 5 minutes until it thickens slightly and turns glossy. Brush this over the last 20 minutes on the smoker — the glaze will caramelize lightly and give the jerky a beautiful sheen and that sweet-salty punch. Pull them when they're still a little flexible; they'll firm up as they cool.

Let the jerky rest on a wire rack for an hour before packaging. If you're gifting it, toss the slices in a zip-top bag with a small packet of silica desiccant to keep it crisp. Store at room temperature for up to 2 weeks — if it lasts that long.

💡 Pro Tips

Slice against the grain for a more tender bite. Slicing with the grain gives you longer fibers that can feel chewier. Keep your smoker humidity in check — if your smoke is moving slowly and white instead of thin and blue, you're good. A water pan near the firebox helps stabilize temp and keeps the jerky from drying out too fast on the edges.
